# Pattern: Corporate Knowledge Hub

A central, searchable place for an organization's scattered internal knowledge (wikis,
documents, tribal know-how) so employees find answers faster — often raised around slow
onboarding, repeated questions, or knowledge lost when people leave.

## Typical Business Goals
- Reduce time-to-answer / time-to-productivity for new and existing staff
- Cut repeated questions to experts; preserve knowledge before key people leave
- Make existing documentation findable rather than producing more of it

## Common Stakeholders
- Knowledge/content owners (per domain), team leads, subject-matter experts
- HR / onboarding (if framed around ramp-up), IT/data owners for the source systems
- End users (the staff searching), an executive sponsor for adoption

## Common Risks
- Source content is outdated, duplicated or contradictory — "garbage in"
- Access/permissions complexity across source systems
- Low adoption: people keep asking colleagues instead of using the hub
- Effort to maintain content ownership after launch is underestimated

## Common Assumptions (to validate)
- That the knowledge mostly exists and is just hard to find (vs. genuinely undocumented)
- That content owners have time to curate and keep entries current
- That search relevance alone drives adoption

## Common Pitfalls (experience — validate)
- Frequently the real problem is **undocumented tribal knowledge**, not search — a hub
  cannot surface what was never written down.
- Often **content governance/ownership** (who keeps it current) is the harder problem than
  the technology.
- Adoption is commonly overestimated; without a workflow that pulls people to the hub it
  is often bypassed.

## Success Factors
- A named owner per knowledge domain and a lightweight content-freshness process
- A few high-value, high-traffic use cases first (not "all knowledge at once")
- Embedding the hub into existing workflows where the questions actually arise
